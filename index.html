Full Writeup

About the Product

InspectAR (originally by the company of the same name) takes the electronic design automation (EDA) design files for printed circuit boards (PCB's) and calibrates those designs to a physical board. Users can then overlay the information they need directly onto the board via AR to aid debug and repair. That info is augmented through component datasheets and collaboration tools. Available on PC (previously also on Mac/Android/iOS), used by hobbyists and Fortune 500 company engineers alike.

Major Contributions

  • Version 2.0 UI Revamp - Completely reworked the UI to be modern and conform to platform design sensibilities. I made a dedicated UI for desktop mode, paying attention to expected button dimensions, information density, and subwindow paradigms unavailable on mobile. I’m not an electrical engineer, so this process included team member consultation, independent research, modeling from the original framework, figma mockups with reviews. Major features required iconography, some of which I created fresh.
  • Attachments - Allows users to append component data sheets, diagrams, and other documentation to a projects. I was in charge of a fullstack implementation: Data model design, a database migration script, additional REST API’s in golang and C# for CRUD operations, and client UI.
  • Issue Management - Allows reporting of problems and solutions for a run of circuit boards. Data model had to balance ease of filtration and retrieval within database but have the right allowances for customers to define their workflow’s trackables. Furthermore, client-side rendering and CRUD GUI had to be similarly comprehensive. I offered numerous possible implementations at different levels of scope, covering ambiguities in the Product team’s presentations.

Skills Demonstrated

  • UX Design - Every feature was designed around how an end user would interpret the information on screen. Prioritization of interactions, grouping icons, tracking overall interaction flows (how many places might a user might wish to flag a component with a comment?). Created a tutorial project with systems to funnel users through interaction and track completion metrics.
  • UI Design - I wove in design practices from Google, Microsoft, and Atlassian to ensure data is presented in a readable and familiar way. Our app is highly specialized, so every new feature needed extra consideration to bridge internal convention and external sensibilities. Moreover, those information-heavy desktop features had to be convenient on mobile platforms.
  • Programming - Being the sole developer on the client, I was responsible for a wide variety of software engineering tasks. Some data models were dictated by our backend developer(s), others required fashioning from a collection of business requirements.
  • Technical Writing - The Product team would prioritize which features or improvements were the major milestone to include in a given release. As mentioned above, I was tasked with writing plan documents to cover implementation details and timelines to ensure our departments were on the same page.
  • Quality Assurance - Before a given release, I was responsible for managing the QA process. I coordinated testcases for changes to the product, a series of automated regression tests, coordination of team efforts on testing, and patches during the testing process.
  • Collaboration - Teamwork took many forms over the years. When I initially joined, I worked under the original programmer for the Unity app. He handled the core systems and I focused on supporting content improvments. When I took over for my predecessor, I had to handle the design meetings with Product and Management - raising concerns, offering alternative implementations, ensuring everyone is best informed on our options. In 2025, I was responsible for a direct report, and it took time to work out how to reconcile our pace and strategic variances.

Working at Adknown

Adknown is primarily an advertising agency, but boasted a small video game division while I worked there. Each game used a UI template (handling basic api calls such as managing save data or switching to a shop screen). The expectation was game design, progression, content, and publication were to be handled by us (ie. 4 developers, making one game each). In practice, even the art and audio had to be created and heavily modified to work in this context. Games had to be published to both storefronts within the ten business days. There were restrictions about making games unique from each other while all being sufficiently "action oriented". Beyond these rules, I pushed for unique genres and dynamic progression systems as a point of pride.

Highlights

  • 22 games in all - I made a snapshot reel to demo the games at a glance
  • Song Pong - Art drawn from scratch in a vaporwave aesthetic, pushed for complex mission tracking such as note streaks and combo values to mimic genre conventions, included a daily check-in system, balancing chords of notes to work flipped or opposing colors to increase replay value, sourced some of the music used from a local musician. Explaining this game in particular helped me get hired at InspectAR!
  • Let’s Get Kraken - Art drawn from scratch, significant balance alterations available as shop items (such as number of tentacles or spawn rates), fair complexity in gameplay balance

Skills Demonstrated

  • Design - Concepting the game and mocking it up on paper for managerial approval.
  • Programming - Beyond a template structure to handle UI screens and save data, all gameplay was programmed by me. Game loop, balance, AI, progression, and mission objectives were developed and iterated on to be challenging and compelling.
  • Art - We had access to the Unity Asset Store, but were responsible for the implementation of all art. I did my own 3D modeling/rigging/texturing/animation, sometimes 2D art/animation, but regularly modified any art assets to support the game's theme and design.
  • Sound - Mostly the modification of sound effects/background music to keep things cohesive and punchy
  • Publishing - I created marketing materials, such as app store imagery and SSO-based copy, to help the game become successful organically.
  • Teamwork - Beyond design advice and programming assistance, I also helped keep my peers on track by coordinating and fulfilling some of the administrative publication steps.
